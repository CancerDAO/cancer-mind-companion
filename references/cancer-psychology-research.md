# Anxiety and Depression Prevalence in Cancer Patients: Research Summary

## Global Prevalence Overview

Depression and anxiety are among the most common psychiatric comorbidities in cancer patients. An estimated **one in three cancer patients** experiences clinically significant symptoms of anxiety or depression. These conditions are under-recognized and undertreated in oncology settings.

**Key summary figures:**
- Depression affects **up to 20%** of cancer patients at any point in the cancer trajectory (vs. 5% in the general population)
- Anxiety affects **up to 10%** of cancer patients (vs. 7% in the general population)
- When using self-report instruments with clinical cutoffs, moderate-to-severe depressive symptom rates range from **13–27%**
- Using diagnostic interview criteria, current major depression prevalence is approximately **14.3%** in newly diagnosed cancer patients
- For anxiety (diagnostic criteria): approximately **10%** of cancer patients

**Source:** Butow P et al. Depression and anxiety in patients with cancer. *BMJ*. 2016;361:k1415. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.k1415

## Meta-Analysis: Depression and Anxiety Prevalence (Global)

### Depression in Cancer Survivors
- **Global summary prevalence: 33.16%** (95% CI: 27.59–38.74)
- Pain prevalence: 39.77% post-treatment; 65.22% before treatment; 51.34% during treatment
- During COVID-19 pandemic: depression rose to **43.25%** (95% CI: 41.25–45.26)

**Source:** PMC11841195 — Global prevalence and determinant factors of pain, depression, and anxiety among cancer patients: an umbrella review of systematic reviews and meta-analyses. *BMC Cancer*. 2025.

### Anxiety in Cancer Survivors
- **Global summary prevalence: 30.55%** (95% CI: 24.04–37.06)
- During COVID-19 pandemic: anxiety rose to **52.93%** (95% CI: 50.91–54.96)
- Men: 20% prevalence (95% CI: 15–24%)
- Women: **31%** prevalence (95% CI: 28–34%) — nearly one-third higher than men
- Highest prevalence by cancer type: ovarian (43%), breast (26%), lung (24%)

**Source:** Frontiers in Psychiatry 2024 — Prevalence of anxiety symptoms/disorders in cancer patients. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2024.1422540

### Large Cross-Sectional Study: 7,509 Cancer Outpatients
- **Anxiety symptoms: 35.2%** (n=2642); significant symptoms indicating very likely anxiety condition: 16.2% (n=1220)
- **Depressive symptoms: 27.9%** (n=2229); significant symptoms indicating very likely depression: 15% (n=1122)
- Women significantly more affected for anxiety (20.6% vs. 11.7% in men)
- Lowest depression rates: breast cancer patients
- Highest anxiety and depression: lung and brain cancer patients

**Source:** Hinz A et al. Prevalence of anxiety and depression in people with different types of cancer or haematologic malignancies. *Psycho-Oncology*. 2022. PMC9583630. PMID: 36245424

### Cancer Types with Highest Burden
| Cancer Type | Anxiety | Depression |
|-------------|---------|------------|
| Lung | Highest | Highest |
| Brain | Highest | Highest |
| Ovarian | Very high | High |
| Breast | Moderate | Lowest (of major cancers) |
| Hematologic | Moderate | Moderate |

**Source:** Hinz et al. 2022; Frontiers in Psychiatry 2024

## Risk Factors for Depression and Anxiety in Cancer Patients

### Cancer-Specific Factors
- Advanced cancer stage (OR = 3.8 for depression; OR = 5.44 for anxiety vs. early stage)
- Type of cancer (lung, brain, ovarian highest burden)
- Treatment phase (highest during active treatment and diagnosis)

### Biological Factors
- Pain (bidirectional relationship with depression and anxiety)
- Sleep disturbances
- Fatigue
- Chemotherapy and corticosteroids (associated with depression as side effects)
- Some cancers (pancreatic, lung) may release chemicals that directly cause depression

### Psychological and Social Factors
- Poor social support
- Prior psychiatric history (small effect size — cancer itself is a decisive factor)
- Lower education level (OR = 2.57 for depression)
- Financial strain / lack of financial support (OR = 1.47–2.88)
- Younger age (associated with more anxiety)
- Older age (associated with more depression)

**Source:** PMC10980245 — A systematic review of risk factors associated with depression and anxiety in cancer patients; BMC Psychiatry 2024. https://doi.org/10.1007/s12888-024-06389-5

## Psychological Distress in Psycho-Oncology Clinical Practice

### Real-World Clinical Data
In a study of 1,048 initial psycho-oncological consultations (Germany, 2025):
- **74.7%** of patients scored above clinical distress cutoff (distress score ≥ 5/10)
- **44.5%** rated as clinically anxious
- **28.6%** rated as clinically depressed
- **25%** received a formal mental health diagnosis (mostly adjustment disorders or affective disorders)
- Patients with non-curative prognosis showed higher anxiety and depression than those with curative prognosis
- Anxiety about disease progression was highest in patients aged 40–65

**Source:** Journal of Cancer Research and Clinical Oncology. 2026. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00432-025-06419-z

## ASCO Guideline Data (2023 Update)

### Key Statistics from ASCO
- 12-month prevalence of any mental disorder in cancer patients: **OR 1.28** compared to general population controls
- Depression rates in cancer (self-report instruments, cutoff ≥ 10): **13–27%**
- Depression rates in cancer (diagnostic criteria): **14.3%**
- Anxiety (diagnostic criteria): approximately **10%**
- Among cancer patients with anxiety disorder, **31% also have major depressive disorder**
- GAD is the most prevalent anxiety disorder in this population

**Source:** Rowland JH et al. Management of Anxiety and Depression in Adult Survivors of Cancer: ASCO Guideline Update. *J Clin Oncol*. 2023. https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.23.00293

## Comparison with General Population

| Condition | Cancer Patients | General Population |
|-----------|----------------|-------------------|
| Depression | ~14–33% | ~4.4% (WHO global) |
| Anxiety | ~10–30% | ~3.6% (WHO global) |
| 12-month mental disorder | OR 1.28 vs. controls | — |

Depression rates are **at least 2× higher** in cancer patients than in the general population.

**Source:** WHO global estimates cited in ASCO 2023 Guidelines; BMJ 2016

## Why These Conditions Are Under-Recognized

1. **Symptom overlap:** Fatigue, insomnia, appetite changes, and low energy are symptoms of both cancer/treatment and depression/anxiety — making differentiation difficult
2. **Normalization:** Distress is often dismissed as a "normal reaction" to cancer
3. **Stigma:** Patients may fear being seen as "difficult" or worry that mental health concerns will affect their cancer care
4. **Geographical variation:** Diagnosis and treatment rates vary widely, indicating systematic under-recognition, especially in low-resource settings
5. **Healthcare system barriers:** Limited psycho-oncology workforce and referral networks

---

## Impact of Untreated Anxiety and Depression in Cancer

- **Reduced treatment adherence:** Depressed/anxious patients are less likely to follow treatment plans
- **Poorer cancer survival outcomes:** Depression associated with reduced survival
- **Increased suicide risk:** Significantly elevated in all cancer patients
- **Higher healthcare costs and utilization**
- **Worse quality of life** across all domains

**Source:** BMJ 2016; ASCO 2023; JAMA Internal Medicine 2006 (GAD-7 validation study)
