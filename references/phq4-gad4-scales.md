# PHQ-4 and GAD-4 Screening Scales

## PHQ-4 (Patient Health Questionnaire-4)

**Developed by:** Kroenke K, Spitzer RL, Williams JBW, Löwe B (2009)
**Original publication:** *Psychosomatics* 50(6):613-621
**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.psy.50.6.613

### Purpose
The PHQ-4 is an **ultra-brief self-report screening tool** for anxiety and depression, combining the PHQ-2 (depression subscale) and GAD-2 (anxiety subscale). It is designed for rapid screening in clinical settings where time is limited. It is **not a diagnostic tool** — elevated scores indicate the need for further assessment.

### Items
**Over the last 2 weeks, how often have you been bothered by the following problems?**

Response options: Not at all (0) | Several days (1) | More than half the days (2) | Nearly every day (3)

| # | Question | Subscale |
|---|----------|----------|
| 1 | Feeling nervous, anxious, or on edge | Anxiety (GAD-2) |
| 2 | Not being able to stop or control worrying | Anxiety (GAD-2) |
| 3 | Little interest or pleasure in doing things | Depression (PHQ-2) |
| 4 | Feeling down, depressed, or hopeless | Depression (PHQ-2) |

### Scoring

**Total Score (0–12):**
| Score | Category |
|-------|----------|
| 0–2 | None (Normal) |
| 3–5 | Mild |
| 6–8 | Moderate |
| 9–12 | Severe |

**Anxiety Subscale (Items 1+2, score 0–6):**
- Score ≥ 3 = positive screen for anxiety disorder; recommend GAD-7 follow-up

**Depression Subscale (Items 3+4, score 0–6):**
- Score ≥ 3 = positive screen for depression; recommend PHQ-9 follow-up

### Psychometric Properties
- **Internal consistency:** α = 0.72–0.88 (total); depression subscale α = 0.65–0.81; anxiety subscale α = 0.74–0.84
- **Two-factor structure** confirmed (Anxiety and Depression factors), invariant across gender, age, geography, income, education, and language
- **Sensitivity:** ~90% for both PHQ-2 and GAD-2 at cutoff ≥ 3
- **Specificity:** ~61% for both subscales
- Higher PHQ-4 scores are strongly associated with functional impairment and disability days

### Clinical Use Notes
- Takes 1–2 minutes to complete
- Administer when screening indicates concern, or at routine intervals
- A positive screen does **not** equal a diagnosis — always follow up with clinical interview
- Further evaluation: PHQ-9 (full depression) or GAD-7 (full anxiety) for patients who screen positive
- Available in many languages at: https://www.phqscreeners.com

## GAD-7 (Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7)

**Developed by:** Spitzer RL, Kroenke K, Williams JBW, Lowe B (2006)
**Original publication:** *Archives of Internal Medicine* 166(10):1092-1097
**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1001/archinte.166.10.1092

### Purpose
The GAD-7 is a 7-item self-report scale for assessing the severity of generalized anxiety disorder. It is the most widely used brief anxiety measure in clinical practice and research. It is also sensitive to symptoms of social phobia, PTSD, and panic disorder.

### Items
**Over the last 2 weeks, how often have you been bothered by the following problems?**

Response options: Not at all (0) | Several days (1) | More than half the days (2) | Nearly every day (3)

| # | Question |
|---|----------|
| 1 | Feeling nervous, anxious, or on edge |
| 2 | Not being able to stop or control worrying |
| 3 | Worrying too much about different things |
| 4 | Trouble relaxing |
| 5 | Being so restless that it's hard to sit still |
| 6 | Becoming easily annoyed or irritable |
| 7 | Feeling afraid as if something awful might happen |

### Scoring

**Total Score (0–21):**
| Score | Severity |
|-------|----------|
| 0–4 | Minimal anxiety |
| 5–9 | Mild anxiety |
| 10–14 | Moderate anxiety |
| 15–21 | Severe anxiety |

**Clinical cutoffs:**
- **Score ≥ 10:** Recommended cutoff for identifying probable GAD cases (sensitivity 89%, specificity 82%)
- Scores of 5, 10, and 15 represent mild, moderate, and severe levels, analogous to PHQ-9 depression severity

### GAD-2 (Ultra-Brief Version — First 2 Items)
The first 2 items of the GAD-7 constitute the GAD-2, which can be used as a standalone ultra-brief anxiety screener:
- **Score 0–2:** Negative screen
- **Score 3–6:** Probable anxiety disorder; recommend full GAD-7 follow-up
- Score ≥ 3 on GAD-2 = positive screening result

### Psychometric Properties
- **Area under ROC curve:** 0.906
- Items had the highest correlation with total 13-item scale score (r = 0.75–0.85)
- The first 3 items capture the DSM-IV core criteria (criteria A and B) for GAD
- 6 of 7 items showed greater divergent validity from depression than would be expected by chance
- Internal consistency: excellent (Cronbach's α reported in validation studies)

### Clinical Use Notes
- The GAD-7 is based on DSM-IV criteria but is also sensitive to other common anxiety disorders
- Scores should be verified by clinical interview; the tool does not provide a diagnosis
- A score of 10 or greater on the GAD-7 is the recommended cutoff for further evaluation
- The scale is also useful for monitoring symptom change over time

## GAD-4 — Note

There is no independently published "GAD-4" scale. The 4-item version sometimes referenced in literature is the **GAD-2** combined with two additional items, or it may be confused with the PHQ-4 (which contains a GAD-2 subscale). When referring to a 4-item anxiety measure, the correct terminology is either **GAD-2** (2 items) or the anxiety subscale of the **PHQ-4** (items 1–2 = GAD-2). For clinical and research use, the validated tools are the **GAD-7** (7 items) or **GAD-2** (2 items).
