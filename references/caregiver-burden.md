# Cancer Caregiver Burden and the Zarit Burden Interview

## Overview

Family caregivers of cancer patients provide essential daily care, including symptom management, medication administration, transportation, emotional support, and coordination of care. This caregiving role often imposes significant physical, emotional, financial, and social burden — frequently referred to as **caregiver burden** or **caregiver burnout**.

**Key fact:** Among caregivers of incurable cancer patients, **59% experience clinically significant caregiver burden** as measured by the Zarit Burden Interview.

**Source:** Al-Rawash AS et al. Caregiver burden among family caregivers of incurable cancer patients: a cross-sectional study from Eastern Mediterranean. *BMC Palliat Care*. 2021. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12904-021-00857-5

## Signs and Symptoms of Caregiver Burnout

### Emotional Signs
- Persistent feelings of exhaustion, hopelessness, or helplessness
- Increased irritability, anger, or resentment toward the patient or situation
- Feeling isolated, alone, or misunderstood
- Sadness, grief, or depressive symptoms
- Anxiety about the patient's condition or about being unable to provide adequate care
- Guilt about having negative feelings toward the patient

### Physical Signs
- Fatigue that does not improve with rest
- Sleep disturbances (insomnia or oversleeping)
- Changes in appetite or weight
- Physical symptoms without clear medical cause (headaches, body aches)
- Neglect of personal health and medical appointments

### Behavioral Signs
- Withdrawing from friends, family, or social activities
- Loss of interest in hobbies or activities previously enjoyed
- Difficulty concentrating or making decisions
- Increased use of alcohol, tobacco, or other substances
- Taking out frustration on others or becoming overly critical

### Role-Related Signs
- Feeling "on duty" 24/7 with no respite
- Inability to take breaks or engage in self-care
- Neglecting other family responsibilities
- Feeling that caregiving is consuming one's entire identity

## The Zarit Burden Interview (ZBI)

### Description
The **Zarit Burden Interview (ZBI)** is the most widely used and best-validated self-report instrument for measuring caregiver burden. It was originally developed by Steven H. Zarit in 1980 as a 29-item questionnaire and later revised to a **22-item version (ZBI-22)**.

### Versions Available
| Version | Items | Administration Time | Use Case |
|---------|-------|-------------------|----------|
| ZBI-22 | 22 | 10–15 min | Standard full assessment |
| ZBI-12 | 12 | 5 min | Brief screening |
| ZBI-7 | 7 | 2–3 min | Ultra-brief; palliative care settings |
| ZBI-1 | 1 | <1 min | Ultra-brief; proxy rating |

**Optimal short version:** A 2018 study found the 6-item version had the best balance of diagnostic utility and brevity, but the **ZBI-12** is most commonly used short form.

**Source:** Yu YP et al. 2018 (6-item optimal version); Zarit Burden Interview — APA Practice Settings. https://www.apa.org/pi/about/publications/caregivers/practice-settings/assessment/tools/zarit

### Items and Scoring (ZBI-22)
Each item is rated on a 5-point scale:
- **0 = Never**
- **1 = Rarely**
- **2 = Sometimes**
- **3 = Quite Frequently**
- **4 = Nearly Always**

Item 22 (overall level of burden) is rated 0–4 from "not at all" to "extremely."

**Total Score Range: 0–88**

### Severity Interpretation (ZBI-22)
| Score Range | Severity Level |
|-------------|----------------|
| 0–20 | Little or no burden |
| 21–40 | Mild to moderate burden |
| 41–60 | Moderate to severe burden |
| 61–88 | Severe burden |

**Clinical cutoff for significant burden: ≥ 21** (used in most cancer caregiver studies)

**Note:** The original author (Zarit) did not provide official cutoffs. The above thresholds are commonly cited in the literature and should be used as guidelines, not absolutes.

**Source:** Zarit Burden Interview — APA; Zarit 1980; Hérbert R et al. 2000; BMC Palliat Care 2021

### ZBI-12 Cutoff
For the 12-item version, a cutoff of **≥ 13** is suggested for community-dwelling caregivers screening positive for burden.

**Source:** BMC Geriatrics 2016 — Brief version of ZBI for older caregivers. PMC6497029

### ZBI-7 in Palliative Care
In a 2020 validation study for palliative care settings, the ZBI-7 showed strong validity as a brief measure. The ZBI-1 (single item on overall burden) was found to have utility as a proxy rating, though it tends to overestimate burden.

**Source:** Kühnel MB et al. Validation of two short versions of the ZBI in the palliative care setting. *Support Care Cancer*. 2020. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00520-020-05471-4

## Prevalence of Caregiver Burden in Cancer

### General Cancer Caregiver Burden
- Studies using ZBI-22 with cutoff ≥ 21 report burden prevalence ranging from **37–82%** in caregivers of cancer patients
- The median/most common estimate is approximately **50–60%**
- A 2021 multi-country study (Egypt and Saudi Arabia) found **59%** of caregivers of incurable cancer patients had significant burden
- In the Middle East/Eastern Mediterranean region: caregivers of advanced cancer patients showed mean ZBI-22 score of **23.4 (SD 9.3)**

**Source:** Al-Rawash AS et al. 2021. BMC Palliat Care. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12904-021-00857-5

### Caregiver Burden by Caregiving Level
In the Al-Rawash 2021 study, burden by level of assistance with Activities of Daily Living (ADLs):
- Level 1 (early-loss ADLs): mean ZBI-22 = **15.5**
- Level 2 (middle-loss ADLs): mean ZBI-22 = **19.9**
- Level 3 (late-loss ADLs): mean ZBI-22 = **25.2**

Only assistance with **late-loss ADLs** was independently associated with significant burden in logistic regression (OR = 3.4, 95% CI: 1.2–9.7, p = 0.024).

**Source:** Al-Rawash AS et al. 2021

### Predictors of Higher Burden
- Late-loss ADL assistance (strongest predictor)
- Best supportive care / palliative treatment intent
- Poor patient performance status
- Lower quality of patient care (per caregiver perception)
- Caregiver's own psychological distress (depression, anxiety)
- Poor caregiver sleep quality
- Lack of help from friends and relatives
- Financial strain
- Caregiver's younger age (in some studies)

**Source:** BMC Palliat Care 2021; BMC Geriatrics 2016; Psycho-Oncology 2015

## Impact of Caregiver Burden

### On the Caregiver
- **Poorer physical health** and immune function
- **Depression and anxiety** — caregivers of cancer patients show elevated rates
- **Reduced quality of life** across all domains
- **Social isolation** and relationship strain
- **Financial toxicity** from caregiving-related costs and lost work
- **Compassion fatigue and burnout**

### On the Patient
- Caregiver burden is associated with **reduced quality of patient care**
- Burned-out caregivers may inadvertently contribute to **poorer patient outcomes**
- Caregiver distress can increase patient distress ( dyadic effect)

**Source:** BMC Palliat Care 2021; Psycho-Oncology 2015; Support Care Cancer 2020

## Evidence-Based Interventions for Caregiver Burden

### Psychosocial Interventions
- **Cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT)** for caregivers — moderate effect on burden reduction
- **Psychoeducation programs** — improve caregiver knowledge and coping
- **Support groups** — reduce isolation and provide practical advice
- **Mindfulness-based interventions (MBI)** — reduce stress and improve well-being
- **Problem-solving therapy** — helps caregivers manage daily challenges

### System-Level Interventions
- **Respite care** — provides temporary relief from caregiving
- **Care coordination services** — reduces caregiver administrative burden
- **Financial counseling** — addresses economic stressors

### Evidence Note
A 2025 prospective observational study found that caregiver burden in palliative cancer care is stable over time (at 1-month follow-up), suggesting that burden is chronic and persistent, and that **interventions need to be repeated or sustained** rather than delivered as single episodes.

**Source:** Slideshare — Caregiver Burden Among Family Caregivers of Cancer Patients Under Palliative Care: Prospective Observational Study (Karnataka, 2024); BMC Palliat Care 2021
